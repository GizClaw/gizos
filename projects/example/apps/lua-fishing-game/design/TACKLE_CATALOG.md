## Game data implications

- Use Brand + Series + Model + Generation/market + Source URL as identity; retain manufacturer product code/JAN where available. Do not fill unknown actions, weights or capacities with invented official values.
- Keep manufacturer specs separate from simulation tuning (stiffness, damping, drag coefficient, release timing). Choosing a real model selects its fixed length/power/action; arbitrary stat cycling should be a separate custom-rod feature.
- Power is not Action. Some makers use blended power labels (LML, ML/M), proprietary ratings (Megabass F-number), or line classes (fly WT). Preserve the source label and map simulation behavior separately.
- Authentic sea-bass rods can exceed the earlier 8 ft limit (morethan example 2.62 m). Full-size ISO rods can be 5.0–5.3 m; do not relabel them 9–10 ft.
- A-WA is a float, not the bait itself. If retained in LURES for interface simplicity, represent it internally as a float rig. ISO rigs in reality also include line, leader, sinkers, hook and bait.
- Topwater poppers/walkers remain visible at the surface; only submerged tackle should become a silhouette. A floating minnow may dive while retrieved and rise during pauses. Existing blanket idle-shadow rendering is a visual placeholder to refine using buoyancy/depth.
- Fly compatibility requires rod WT + line WT + reel capacity. The example SALT R8 8WT and Hydros III 5–7WT are separate catalog entries, not a recommended matching set.

- Reel visual classes: Spinning, Low-profile Baitcasting, Round Baitcasting, Fly. CALCUTTA CONQUEST (CQ) is Round Baitcasting and needs its own drum-shaped geometry above a casting rod. Its silhouette must remain distinct from a fly reel mounted below the rod butt. CQ standard, DC and BFS are different product variants; never mix their specifications.
